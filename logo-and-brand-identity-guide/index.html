What a Good Logo and Brand Identity Should Include

Branding5 min readBy Noyob InnovativePublished 19 October 2026Updated 19 October 2026

A logo is often the first thing a new business asks for, but a strong brand is more than a single image. Here is what a good logo and a complete identity should include.

A logo that works everywhere

A good logo is simple, memorable and clear at any size, from a small social media icon to a large signboard. It should still look good in one colour and on both light and dark backgrounds.

Logo variations

Most brands need a main horizontal version, a stacked version, an icon-only version for small spaces, and a single-colour version for stamps, embroidery or print.

A colour palette

Choose a small set of colours with clear roles: a main brand colour, supporting colours, and neutrals for text and backgrounds. Check that text colours have enough contrast to be readable.

Typography

Pick one or two typefaces for headings and body text, and decide how they are used. Consistent type makes materials look professional without extra effort.

Imagery and graphic style

Decide what kind of photography, icons and illustration suit your brand, so that every post, brochure and page feels like it comes from the same business.

Brand guidelines

A short guide that explains how to use the logo, colours and fonts, including spacing and what to avoid. It helps your team, printers and partners keep the brand consistent.

Consistency over time

A brand becomes recognisable through repetition. Use the same look on your website, social media, documents and packaging, and customers will begin to remember you.

Logo versions in everyday use

Think about where your logo will appear: a website header, a social media profile picture, an invoice, a signboard, a uniform, a delivery box. Each needs a different shape or colour version. A professional logo package prepares these in advance so nothing has to be improvised later.

Common logo mistakes

  • Too much detail, which disappears at small sizes.
  • Following a passing trend that will look dated in two years.
  • Using several fonts and many colours.
  • Poor contrast between the logo and its background.
  • Receiving only an image file instead of vector files that can be resized without losing quality.

Keep your brand consistent

Once your identity is ready, use it the same way everywhere. Save the logo files in one shared folder, give your team the colour codes and fonts, and use templates for posts, proposals and presentations. Review your materials every few months and remove anything that no longer matches. Consistency is what turns a good design into a brand people recognise and trust.

What you should receive

  • Vector logo files in colour, black and white versions.
  • PNG files with transparent backgrounds for web and social media.
  • Your colour codes and font names.
  • A short brand guideline document.
  • Social media profile and cover templates.

Questions to ask before you hire a designer

  • Can I see projects similar to mine, and what was the result?
  • How many concepts and revision rounds are included?
  • Which files will I own at the end, and are vector files included?
  • Will you explain the thinking behind the design, not only show the final logo?
  • Can you also design the website, social media templates and print materials so everything matches?

How long does a branding project take?

A logo and basic identity usually takes two to six weeks, depending on the number of concepts, feedback rounds and extra items such as stationery, packaging and social media templates. Clear feedback and quick approvals keep the schedule short.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a logo and a brand identity?

A logo is a single symbol or wordmark. A brand identity is the whole system around it: colours, typography, imagery, tone of voice and the rules for using them together.

How many logo concepts should I expect?

A focused set of concepts, followed by refinement of the one you choose. Quality and fit matter more than a large number of options.

Do I need brand guidelines for a small business?

A short guide is valuable even for small businesses. It keeps your look consistent when different people create your posts, print materials and website pages.

Can you redesign my existing logo instead of starting over?

Yes. A refresh can keep the recognition you have built while modernising shapes, colours and typography.
